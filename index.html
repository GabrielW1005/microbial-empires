FIELD GUIDE

Gather. Build. Grow.

  1. Give workers a job. Click a worker, then right-click brown food or gray minerals. It gathers, returns to the colony, and repeats. Send two to food and one to minerals.
  2. Grow gradually. Select the home colony and queue workers. Select a worker to build an algal bed for more space and a little food. Then construct a grazing pool for ciliates.
  3. Expand across the pond. Mature your colony to unlock amoebae. Protect your workers and destroy the rival home colony to win. The rival launches no raids during the first five minutes.

On a touchpad, select a unit, click its Gather, Move, or Attack move button, then click the destination. Build buttons work the same way.

DISPLAY

Keep it light.

Shaded sprites and terrain are drawn once and reused. A limit of 48 units per side keeps the simulation small. Display settings do not change game speed.
BIOLOGY & FIELD NOTES

A pond, interpreted.

Color with a reason.

Many unstained bacteria and freshwater protists are almost colorless. Here they use pale gray and cream with stronger outlines so they remain readable. Green is reserved for chloroplast-bearing algae. Brown patches represent decomposing organic matter; blue and red marks are player identifiers, not cell pigments.

Recognizable organisms.

Rod-shaped bacteria gather resources. Ciliates have short surface hairs and a visible nucleus. Amoebae change shape using pseudopodia and surround smaller prey. These are simplified functional types; actual diets vary between species.

A strategy game, not a laboratory model.

Workers carrying minerals, construction, production queues, teams, combat, and maturation are explicit RTS abstractions. Sizes, times, nutrient rates, and algal food production are compressed for play. The model does not simulate water flow, calibrated chemistry, or a conserved microbial food web.

One map, one AI opponent, no fog of war. Original shaded sprite artwork. No third-party runtime assets, advertising, tracking, or accounts.
